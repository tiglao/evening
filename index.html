a letter, before dinner

at the table again

on tanám, on repair, and on a season of monday nights.

by elliecambridge, mass.

hello, friend 👋🏽

this is an invitation to a table (you remember the one?). from october through december, a loose collective of chefs, bartenders and artists of color is making it happen on monday nights, somewhere in cambridge, for ten to twenty people at a time. the dates are below. mark the ones you want.

i.before

until recently, i only spoke of tanám in the past tense. the pop up i started twelve years ago called pamangan created time and space that let me meander towards the a-ha moment that seeded tanám. and in 2019, a home was built for the food/art/social project that also happened to serve filipinx american food.

in 2020, a pandemic happened and tanám had to unfortunately become a restaurant. and in the last month of 2022, two of our most important people left together -- the rest of us decided to close in 2023. obviously, this is an ultra abridged accounting.

but there are so many things i loved about that space, more than anything, how we didn't shy away from the ugly, how we embraced the silly in our seriousness (ask me about the annual red horse run). i miss it. i'm also relieved i'm no longer bound to that space. both are true.

after the restaurant closed, i realized just how difficult it had been to spend time with loved ones both near and far, but also began to understand that what we were doing through lockdown and beyond was just a shadow of the original intent. tanám didn't live there anymore.

this year, building coop social club and gathering the artists of red sun studios, i kept reaching for what we learned at tanám, but especially what brought olio together, the worker co-op that a bunch of community organizers (myself included) started to facilitate their community food work. then, on a perfect summer night in august this year, i was invited to remember.

it's been a while, but i've always known. tanám is my core, my heart.

three years after we closed, i'm ready to tend to what was left. a complex and layered history happened around a table, with people i loved, while we tried to make food and art pay the bills. i know staying away from this work is not the way through. the path forward is at the table, with other people, over food and drink, and through art.

ii.the table

this series is built around the people making it. each night belongs to whoever's leading it: a cook, a bartender, an artist, sometimes all three (and sometimes all the same person!), making the thing they've been wanting to make. their curiosity sets the menu. their joy is the point.

you're invited into that process while it's still a draft. you might taste a dish on its second try, or a drink that gets renamed halfway through the night. the people making it will sit down and eat with you, compare notes, and chit chat about whatever arises. what happens at the table shapes what comes next.

no two nights run the same. a meal might be coursed or passed around the table. it might start at six or at midnight, end on time or turn into a dance party. we could see the sunrise together. the point is to just have fun.

iii.the mondays

    the calendar is alive, so nights may shift or change.

    to get a seat, mark every night you'd want. hosts choose their first guests. seats still open a week out go to everyone else who said yes, first come, first served. if you're in, you'll get an email with the time and the address.

    iv.paying for it

    there's no ticket. every monday on the list shows three numbers, and you choose.

    1. the first covers your plate: food, drink, supplies.
    2. the second adds half of what the crew's time is worth.
    3. the third pays everyone who cooked, poured and made the room a fair wage for the night.

    smaller nights cost more per person, so the numbers move. give from where you are. no one is turned away over money, and exchanges are possible (think commrcial kitchen space, storage, something you grew. tell me what you else you've got to offer).

    once a host offers you a seat, a $25 deposit holds it and counts toward what you give. if the night falls through, the deposit rolls forward, comes back to you, or stays with us. your call.

    can't make it but want this to keep going? give anyway. it pays the cooks and the documentation: photos, video, the zine.
