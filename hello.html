october 13, 2026a letter by ellie

hello friend,

i always hoped that when i finally wrote about tanám, after a long public silence, i’d capture my big feelings perfectly. it’s been three and a half years and i’m no closer. every false start spirals into a novella i don’t have time for (...yet!).

‘what’s next?’ people would ask.

well, to spare us all, i'll be concise: i’ve figured out some things, but i'm figuring out how the food x art piece works now. and i’m hoping you’ll be a part of it.

for the next few months, i’ll be spending my free time with other chefs, bartenders and artists of color, bringing people into our worlds. yes, our personal experiences, our histories and politics, our food and drink. but also, our process. the complicated relationships we may have with our craft. how it weaves into the rest of our lives and back again.

we’re each coming to this from different contexts, with different histories and different reasons. wherever we start, we’re learning it together.

what we ask of you: come open, come curious, and show up as yourself. consider this a dinner party.

...but i’ve already said too much! there's room for you at this table.

you can find out more about the upcoming nights, including the proposed (and live!) menus, here.

the first four are mine to host, and i want to make a special plug for the first: death, this upcoming monday, october 19. we start at 11 pm, the part of a wake when the visitors have gone home and the ones staying up with the dead pull their chairs in closer. it’s a night i’ve wanted to make since 2019.

the way death tends to get handled in this country, quietly and kept out of sight, has always felt foreign to me. for filipinos who keep our old traditions, death is something we keep close: we feed our dead, we talk to them, we set food out for them. they’re never really gone. in the old belief, our dead become anito, ancestor spirits who stay close and keep watch over us. our wakes are long, social and full of food. at undas, families spend the night at the cemetery with candles, cards and a feast laid out on the graves, more reunion than mourning. all of it says the same thing: death doesn’t keep us apart.

so i want to push on the idea that death is untouchable, unspeakable. for one night, let’s talk about it out loud, and celebrate the people who’ve passed on the way they deserve: with food, drink and stories.

if there’s one night i’m asking you to come to, it’s this one.

there’s no ticket. instead, every night has three numbers as markers, and you choose whatever you can do for the 19th:

  • $30covers your plate: food, drinks, supplies.
  • $90pays for about half the prep and staff time.
  • $150pays everyone something fair for prep, cooking and the night itself.

the numbers change from night to night, depending on the menu and number of seats. give from where you are. no one is turned away over money.

the 19th is less than a week out, which means seats are first come, first served. to claim one, venmo what you can with “oct 19” in the note, or just write and tell me you’re coming.
